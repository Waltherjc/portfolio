## Introduction

This chair was built for Miyako, a nine-year-old girl with muscular dystrophy. She had recently had surgery to fuse her neck and place rods in her back, which improved her posture but limited her head movement. Before the project she could not shower without her parents helping her.

The aim was a shower chair sized to her, supportive enough to give her independence in a daily routine. The supportive chairs already on the market were either toddler-sized or adult-sized, with nothing in between.

The project was built at the 2025 Vanderbilt TOM (Tikkun Olam Makers) Makeathon, 24–26 October 2025.

## Design

The foundation was an off-the-shelf shower chair ([adjustable anti-slip shower stool](https://www.amazon.com/OasisSpace-Adjustable-Anti-Slip-Disabled-Assembly/dp/B0CYFRMGVQ)). Its adjustable leg height was the deciding feature — the chair can grow with Miyako rather than being outgrown in a year.

PVC pipe was the right material for the supports: stiff enough to carry load, and water resistant. Pool noodles cover the PVC everywhere Miyako contacts the back and arm supports, which makes the seat comfortable without adding anything that traps water.

A mesh shower bag hangs off one arm so soap and shower tools are within reach. Small [hanger hooks](https://www.amazon.com/PCS-Hanger-Connector-Hooks-Organizers/dp/B0DDT1FJM1) screwed to the arm let the bag lift off to be washed.

## Assembly

1. Assemble the base shower chair per its instruction manual.
2. Drill two PVC pipes into the underside of the seat so the back and arm supports tie structurally into the seat.
3. Cut the PVC to length and dry-fit it with connectors to form the back support and two arm supports.
4. Take the dry-fit joints apart, apply PVC glue, and reassemble so the frame cannot separate under load.
5. Screw three small hooks to the lower arm tube, using large washers so the screw heads fully cover the hook openings, and hang the mesh bag.

The back support carries two crossbeams: one at the top tying the two vertical pipes together, and one three-quarters of the way up to support Miyako's upper back. Each arm support takes two connections off its vertical pipe rather than one, spreading the load so a single joint cannot become a failure point.

The hooks are mounted on the lower arm tube specifically so they clear the pool noodle on the upper tube and never sit where she leans.
