## Introduction

The goal of this project was to design and fabricate an oil tank for a Yamaha YFZ450S engine to be used on the VU Motorsports car. The need for a new tank, rather than reusing the OEM one, arose when a new air intake system was created that left the OEM oil tank without a home. Several designs followed as the tank moved around the car.

Three requirements drove the design. The tank had to hold roughly 2 L: engine oil capacity is 1.95 L, with at most 1.55 L sitting in the tank at any one time. It had to be buildable from sheet aluminium, which ruled out curved or intricate surfaces. And it had to sit low in the car — a tank above the engine floods it with oil, while a low tank also drops the centre of mass and improves weight distribution.

## Design

All CAD was done in SolidWorks so the part could drop straight into the full car assembly.

### Location 1 — behind the engine

The first location was behind the engine and ahead of the rear differential: low to the ground and large enough for the required volume. Two problems killed it. The flat bottom stranded a lot of oil below the pickup tubes, which I fixed by angling the floor. Then the team changed the rear differential and the space shrank.

### Location 2 — beside the engine

The tank then moved to the right side of the car, tucked between the engine and the roll cage. The angled floor survived, but new problems appeared. To hit the volume target, the face nearest the roll cage had to become complex enough to make fabrication impractical. The bottom of the tank also became too narrow for the filter on the oil pickup tubes. Worst of all, too much of the tank ended up above the engine, which would have flooded it.

### Location 3 — ahead of the engine

The final location sat ahead of the engine and behind the firewall separating driver from powertrain. Most of the tank fell below the engine, the floor could be pitched, and the base was wide enough for the pickup tube filter. The car battery had been living there, but battery placement is far more flexible than oil tank placement, so it moved.

In this iteration the oil pickup tube sits slightly above the floor to clear a roll cage tube. One side is slanted to match the firewall with 0.25 in of clearance so vibration cannot chafe it, and the outboard face follows the roll cage profile to use every available millimetre without interfering with anything else.

### Cardboard prototype

Working from the finished CAD, I cut each face of the tank out of cardboard to check the measurements, then hot-glued them together in the CAD orientation so nothing could shift during fitting. The measurements were not exact — limited by the measuring tools and the thickness of the cardboard — but the mock-up dropped into the target location with clearance for the surrounding parts.

## Fabrication and outcome

With the cardboard model confirming the measurements, fabrication started in thin sheet aluminium. Each side was cut separately so the angles could be fine-tuned, except the top and back, which were cut as one piece and bent to 90° on a sheet metal brake. Fitting the oil pickup tube meant cutting an elongated, filleted parallelogram out of one face; to avoid compromising the rest of the panel I drilled out the centre and filed back to the line.

The pieces were cut and bent and ready to weld when the car design was overhauled. The new air intake was judged not ready for competition and was replaced with one routed outside the roll cage — which let the OEM oil tank fit again, and that is what the car ran.
